How the maps were generated, solved, and ranked

Exact rules, exact outcomes

One visible tunnel, fixed walls, no powers or modifiers, orthogonal one-square movement. The warder moves first and cannot enter the tunnel. Capture occurs on contact. A player can wait by letting the real game timer expire; such turns are included and labelled. These results do not cover Special-mode powers or changing terrain.

The solver works backwards from every capture and escape. It verifies the outcome and optimal remaining distance at every valid position. Indefinite-avoidance starts are excluded from this showcase. The winner finishes as quickly as possible; the opponent resists for as long as possible. This is one optimal continuation, not the only winning plan. The full state graph establishes the force-of-win claim.

Each stored demonstration is also replayed through the actual Escape Plan rules engine. The independent test suite compares an alternative solver against every 3×3 wall pattern with a fixed exit, then checks larger-board transitions.

Real pool, reproducible selection

SizeStoredW / P winsGeneration

The generator explores eligible starting pairs on each random layout, samples up to three, and alternates easier/middle/harder selections. It prefers the underrepresented winner when that layout allows it. Rotations and reflections of a layout are deduplicated. Demonstrations require 4–80 individual turns. Each size displays 15 examples for each winning role, spaced across the ranked pool.

Difficulty is an explicit hypothesis

Raw score = 40% limited-lookahead difficulty + 25% decision precision + 20% misleading greedy choices + 10% counterintuitive detours + 5% sustained execution length. These weights are design choices, not learned human data.

  • Lookahead: probe 1–5 turns ahead with a fixed distance/mobility heuristic at up to six winning-role decisions. A result of 6 means no successful probe through depth 5; it is not proof that depth 6 is enough.
  • Precision: fraction of legal choices that give up the forced win, averaged over the winner’s turns in this demonstration.
  • Greedy traps: how often the heuristic’s tied first choices give up the forced win.
  • Detours: winning moves that increase distance to the apparent objective.
  • Execution: winner decisions, capped at 15, with only a 5% score weight.

Easy / Medium / Hard are thirds of the score distribution within the same board size and winning role. The displayed percentile is a relative rank, not a win probability or an absolute cross-size difficulty. Features are measured along one optimal line and through one heuristic model; they can miss human insights or alternative branches.

Independent routes use a vertex-disjoint-path calculation. Junctions and loops describe terrain; they are not included in the difficulty score and do not prove safe passage against a moving opponent.
